Research Initiative

The Empathy Engine

A deep dive into redesigning digital feedback loops to feel less like data extraction and more like a genuine conversation.

The Spark

It started with a personal frustration. After a difficult customer service experience, I was emailed a sterile, 10-point scale survey. The company didn't want to hear my story; they wanted a metric to feed their dashboard.

I realized that in our rush to measure user sentiment, we've stripped away the humanity of asking someone, "How was your experience?" This initiated a 3-month personal exploration into conversational UI and emotional design.

"Data extraction feels cold. Conversation feels warm. How do we bridge the gap at scale?"

— Project Journal, Day 1

The Process

I began by analyzing transcripts of actual human-to-human feedback sessions (like a manager talking to an employee). I extracted the cadence: an open question, active listening (acknowledgment), a specific follow-up, and gratitude.

1

Deconstruct

Breaking down human empathy into conversational turns.

2

Prototype

Building interactive Figma prototypes using variable logic to simulate conversation.

3

Test

A/B testing the conversational flow against a traditional form with 50 users.

Lessons Learned

  • Pacing matters more than length. Users were willing to answer MORE questions if they were delivered one at a time in a conversational cadence, rather than presented as a massive block of inputs.
  • Micro-acknowledgments build trust. Simply having the UI say "That sounds frustrating" before asking the next question increased qualitative feedback length by 40%.
